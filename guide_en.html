Strategy Guide: Mastering CoNeutron

Openings, positional principles, and tactics to beat the Hard AI

Inverted Objective Reminder: Unlike classic Neutron, your goal in CoNeutron is to propel the Neutron (yellow) onto your opponent's starting row (row 5 for Blue, row 1 for Red), or to leave your opponent with no legal move.

1. The 3 Core Principles

A. Open Lanes Instead of Building Walls

The most frequent mistake is piling pawns in front of the opponent's back rank. If you block access to the target row, you stop the Neutron from sliding onto it. Always keep clear columns or diagonals pointing toward the enemy base.

B. Controlling the Center (Square C3)

At the start, the Neutron on C3 has 8 sliding directions. As soon as it touches an edge (columns A or E), it loses 3 axes. Occupying the central squares (B3, C3, D3, C2, C4) allows you to redirect the Neutron toward gaps in the opponent's defense.

C. Victory by Suffocation (Blockade)

A player unable to move the Neutron at the start of their turn, or blocked from moving any pawn on their second step, loses immediately. Surrounding the Neutron while keeping one back-rank pawn free is a decisive endgame weapon.

2. Key Openings (Blue's 1st Turn)

On the very first turn of the game, Blue (row 1: A1–E1) moves only one pawn without touching the Neutron:

Opening Initial Move Strategic Objective
Central Opening C1 → C2 (or B1 → B3) Places a "bumper" in front of the Neutron to stop Red from immediately bouncing it back toward your starting row.
Diagonal Wing A1 → A4 (or E1 → E4) Projects a pawn onto the flank to threaten enemy corners (A5 / E5) and set up diagonal rebound angles.
Lateral Lock B1 → D3 (diagonal) Claims a central pivot square while freeing column B for rapid defensive retreats.
Tip against the Hard AI: Avoid advancing both wings (A1→A4 and E1→E4) too early; the AI will exploit the empty squares on your back rank to slide the Neutron there in two turns.

3. Advanced Tactics Against the Hard AI

In Hard mode, the AI searches 3 half-turns ahead (Minimax Alpha-Beta) and evaluates the Neutron's distance to both target rows. Exploit these four maneuvers:

  • The Double Threat (Sliding Fork): Place the Neutron on a square with two clear lines of sight toward the opponent's starting row (e.g., a vertical column and a diagonal). Since the AI can only move one pawn per turn, it can close only one path.
  • The "Bumper" Pawn: Since pieces only stop against an obstacle, infiltrate one of your pawns onto an open square on the opponent's starting row (e.g., C5) so it acts as a stopping block for the Neutron on an adjacent empty square next turn.
  • The Tempo Trap (Zugzwang): The AI consistently tries to push the Neutron toward your side. Deliberately leave an apparent lane open toward an edge (e.g., A2 or E2) where one of your pawns lies in wait to bounce the Neutron back diagonally.
  • Elastic Defense: Never leave more than two adjacent empty squares on your starting row and always keep one mobile back-rank pawn able to slide horizontally.

4. Handling Infinite Loops and Draws

Why infinite games can occur

Unlike games with captures or forward-only pawns, every slide in CoNeutron is reversible. If both players keep 3 or 4 pawns guarding their home row and refuse to open the last critical square, they can repeat a neutral back-and-forth loop indefinitely.

Draw rules applied

Threefold repetition: If the exact same board configuration (positions of all 10 pawns, the Neutron, and the player to move) occurs 3 times during a game, it is declared a draw.
Mutual agreement: Human players may also agree to a draw when neither side can force a breakthrough without losing immediately.

5. Pre-Move Checklist

  1. Immediate safety: If I place the Neutron here, can my opponent slide it onto my starting row on the next turn?
  2. Two-step coupling: Which pawn will I move after the Neutron to shut down the opponent's counter-attack?
  3. Angle opening: Does this move clear a diagonal or column toward the opponent's home row?
