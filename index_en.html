📖 CoNeutron Rules

1. Inverted Goal: Push the Neutron (yellow) onto your opponent's starting row (row 5 for Blue, row 1 for Red).

2. Mandatory Sliding: Every piece (pawns and the Neutron) slides in a straight line (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally) as far as possible until it hits the edge of the board or another piece.

3. Two-Step Turn: On each turn, you must first move the Neutron, and then move one of your own pawns.

4. First-Turn Exception: On the very first turn of the game, Blue moves only one Blue pawn (without moving the Neutron).

5. Stalemate Loss: If a player cannot legally move the Neutron or any of their pawns during their turn, they immediately lose the game.

🎓 Strategy Guide: Mastering CoNeutron

Unlike classic Neutron, your goal in CoNeutron is to propel the Neutron onto your opponent's starting row (row 5 for Blue, row 1 for Red).

1. The 3 Core Principles

  • Open lanes instead of walling: Avoid building a wall in front of the enemy's back rank. Keep clear columns or diagonals pointing toward your target row.
  • Control the center (C3): From C3, the Neutron has 8 slide directions. Once it touches an edge (A or E), it loses 3 directions. Controlling B3, C3, D3, C2, C4 lets you steer the game.
  • Suffocation (Blockade win): Surrounding the Neutron while keeping one back pawn mobile for your second move is often safer than a direct rush.

2. Key Openings (Blue's 1st Turn)

OpeningInitial MoveStrategic Purpose
Central OpeningC1 → C2 (or B1 → B3)Places a "bumper" in front of the Neutron.
Diagonal WingA1 → A4 (or E1 → E4)Threatens enemy corners (A5 / E5) and sets up diagonal rebounds.
Lateral LockB1 → D3Claims a central pivot while freeing column B for defense.
Tip vs. Hard AI: Avoid advancing both wings (A1→A4 and E1→E4) too early; the AI will exploit your empty back rank.

3. Advanced Tactics & Checklist

  • Double Threat (Fork): Place the Neutron on a square with two clear sightlines to the opponent's back rank (e.g., a file and a diagonal).
  • Bumper Pawn: Infiltrate a pawn onto the opponent's back rank (e.g. C5) so it acts as a stopping block for the Neutron on an adjacent empty square.
  • Tempo Trap (Zugzwang): Leave an enticing lane to an edge (e.g. A2/E2) where your pawn waits to bounce the Neutron diagonally back toward their goal.
  • Elastic Defense: Never leave more than two adjacent empty squares on your starting row and keep a mobile back-rank defender.

Pre-move checklist: 1) Can my opponent win next turn? 2) Which pawn will I move right after the Neutron? 3) Does this open a diagonal toward their goal?
